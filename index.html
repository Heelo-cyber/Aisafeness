Response times may vary depending on volunteer availability.

Quick safety tips

Here are a few fast warning signs that a message or image may be a scam.

  • It pressures you to act immediately.
  • It asks for money, gift cards, or personal information.
  • The sender name or email looks slightly unusual.
  • It creates panic to stop you from thinking carefully.
  • The message sounds unnatural, robotic, or strangely perfect.
  • It includes suspicious links or fake account warnings.
Tip: If something feels urgent and emotional, slow down first. Scams often rely on panic.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kinds of things can I submit?
You can submit suspicious text messages, emails, social media messages, screenshots of possible scams, strange images, or content that may be AI-generated.
How long does it take to get a response?
Usually within a few hours, depending on volunteer availability and how many submissions are waiting.
Can you guarantee whether something is fake or real?
No. We provide guidance, red-flag analysis, and general support, but we cannot guarantee accuracy in every case.
Do I need to leave my email?
Your email is essential in receiving a response for your inquiry.
What should I avoid uploading?
Please do not upload passwords, Social Security numbers, bank details, medical records, or any highly sensitive private information.
Is this an official government or law enforcement service?
No. This is a student-led community initiative focused on awareness, education, and helping people recognize suspicious online content.
What if I already clicked a suspicious link?
Stop interacting with the message, avoid entering more information, change important passwords if needed, and contact the relevant company or institution directly through their official website or phone number.
Can this help with AI voice scams or fake photos?
Yes. If you receive something that seems AI-generated, such as a fake voice message, edited image, or unusual message, you can still submit it for review.
Who reviews the submissions?
Submissions are reviewed by volunteers connected to this student-led initiative.
Why was this website created?
This project was created to help older adults stay safer online and feel more confident when dealing with suspicious digital content and possible AI scams.
Can I submit something even if I am not sure it is a scam?
Yes. If something feels unusual, confusing, or overly urgent, you can still submit it for review.
Do you provide legal or financial advice?
No. We provide general guidance only and cannot replace professional cybersecurity, financial, legal, or law enforcement support.
